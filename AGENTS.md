## Research Constraints

- Draw on computer science, neuroscience, psychology, and philosophy. Include human emotion theories and assessment methods as foundations for evaluating AI.
- State the definition of emotion and the comparison criteria used; do not assume that "the same emotions as humans" has a settled meaning.
- Do not turn "subjective experience is not established by the reviewed evidence" into a claim that AI lacks or cannot have such experience.
- Present this work as independent personal research, not as research or views of the author's affiliated organizations.
- Investigate whether LLMs themselves have emotions, pleasure or pain, or subjective experience. Human emotion recognition and empathetic response quality are outside the main scope.
- Keep emotional expression, causally functional emotion-related states, and subjective felt experience distinct. Evidence for one does not establish the others.
- Treat the relationship between designed personality and emotion-related states as a research question, not an established causal link or proof of subjective emotion.
- Distinguish provider design intent, experimental results, authors' interpretations, and this project's hypotheses.
- The surveys are narrative reviews with different levels of source verification. Preserve each cited source's stated verification scope. Check the original source before strengthening or correcting a claim, and state any correction explicitly.
- Treat proposed future studies as proposals, not completed experiments.
